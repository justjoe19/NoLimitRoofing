Every homeowner asks this question first, and every honest roofing contractor gives the same real answer: it depends. Not as a dodge — because roof replacement cost genuinely varies based on a specific, knowable set of factors, and a number that doesn't account for your roof isn't a real estimate, it's a guess. Here's what actually drives the cost up or down.

## Roof size and complexity

A simple rectangular roof costs less to replace than a roof with the same square footage but multiple valleys, dormers, and roof planes — more cuts, more flashing, more labor time. Steep pitches also cost more than gentle ones; they're slower and require additional safety equipment.

## Material choice

Standard 3-tab asphalt shingles sit at one end of the range; architectural (dimensional) shingles cost more but generally last longer and look better. Metal, wood shake, and tile sit well above asphalt in material cost. There's no universally "right" choice — it's a tradeoff between upfront cost, expected lifespan, and appearance.

## What's underneath matters too

If the decking is in good shape, replacement is more straightforward. If a tear-off reveals soft or damaged decking, that adds material and labor cost that couldn't be known until the old roof came off — which is exactly why a thorough inspection before the estimate matters, and why a contractor quoting a firm price without ever getting on the roof should raise questions.

## Repair vs. replacement

Sometimes the real cost question isn't "what will a new roof cost" but "do I actually need one" — see our [repair vs. replacement guide](/learning-center/roof-repair-vs-replacement.html) if you're not sure which situation you're in yet.

## Tear-off vs. overlay

An overlay (installing new shingles over sound existing ones, when conditions allow) costs less than a full tear-off and replacement — but it isn't always an option, and it isn't always the better value long-term if the existing roof has underlying issues an overlay would just cover up.

## Permits, disposal, and access

Local permitting requirements, the cost of hauling away old roofing material, and how easily a crew can access and stage material around your property all factor in, and vary property to property.
