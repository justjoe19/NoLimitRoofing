A roofing estimate that's just a single total number with no detail behind it isn't really an estimate — it's a placeholder that leaves too much room for surprises later. Here's what a written estimate should actually spell out before you sign anything.

## Scope of work

A clear description of exactly what's being done — full tear-off vs. overlay, what's being torn off and down to what layer, what's being installed, and what areas of the roof (or building, for commercial work) are included. Vague language like "roof work as needed" isn't a scope.

## Materials, by name

The specific manufacturer and product line for shingles or roofing membrane, not just "architectural shingles" with no brand — plus underlayment type, ice and water shield placement, flashing material, and ventilation components. If you can't tell what's actually going on your roof from the estimate, ask.

## Decking policy

How decking replacement is handled if damage is found during tear-off — how it's priced, and whether you'll be shown the damage before the work is done and billed. This should be addressed upfront, not left as a surprise line item.

## Timeline

A realistic estimated start date and duration, with an honest note about what could affect it (weather, decking findings, material availability) rather than a date that's more of a hope than a plan.

## Warranty information

What warranty coverage applies — both the manufacturer's material warranty and the contractor's workmanship warranty — and what voids it. Factory certification with a manufacturer (GAF, IKO, Owens Corning, Malarkey, Atlas, CertainTeed, SRS) typically means access to stronger warranty options than a non-certified installer can offer, which is worth confirming.

## Cleanup and disposal

Whether tear-off debris removal and job-site cleanup are included, and what that actually covers (magnetic nail sweep, dumpster/haul-away, etc.).

## Contact and licensing information

The contractor's license, bonding and insurance status should be verifiable, not just claimed. Ask if you're not sure how to confirm it.
