Asphalt and composite shingles are the roofing system on the large majority of homes across South Bend, Mishawaka and the greater Michiana region, and for good reason — they're durable, widely available in a range of colors and styles, and well suited to the region's mix of summer heat, winter snow load and wind. No Limit Roofing installs shingle roofing as a factory-certified contractor with GAF, IKO, Owens Corning, Malarkey and Atlas, which means installation methods verified by the manufacturers themselves and access to stronger warranty options than an uncertified crew can offer.

The shingle itself is only part of what determines how a roof performs. Proper decking, ice and water shield protection in vulnerable areas, correct underlayment, starter strip, ridge protection, drip edge, and — critically — attic ventilation all affect how long a shingle roof actually lasts versus its rated warranty term. A well-installed mid-tier shingle will often outperform a premium shingle installed poorly underneath.

## 3-Tab vs. Architectural Shingles

Two shingle styles cover most residential projects:

- **3-tab shingles** are flat, uniform in appearance and typically the lower-cost option.
- **Architectural (dimensional) shingles** are thicker and layered for a more textured look, and generally carry a longer expected lifespan.

Most homeowners we talk to lean toward architectural shingles for the appearance and the longer expected life, but a 3-tab roof can still be a reasonable fit for some budgets and buildings. If you're also weighing hail resistance, our guide on [architectural vs. impact-resistant shingles](/learning-center/architectural-vs-impact-resistant-shingles.html) explains the real difference.

## What Goes Under The Shingles

Homeowners tend to compare shingle colors and brands, but most of the work that protects your home is out of sight. On a shingle roof, that includes:

1. **Decking.** Solid, dry decking gives the shingles something to hold on to. Soft or damaged decking is replaced before new material goes down. See what happens if [bad decking is found](/learning-center/what-happens-if-bad-decking-is-found.html) during a project.
2. **Ice and water shield.** A self-sealing membrane at the eaves and in valleys where winter meltwater tends to back up. Here's [what ice and water shield does](/learning-center/what-does-ice-and-water-shield-do.html).
3. **Underlayment.** A secondary water barrier over the entire deck.
4. **Drip edge and flashing.** Metal details that direct water off the roof edge and seal it around chimneys, walls and penetrations.
5. **Starter strip and ridge cap.** The edges and peak are where wind tends to get under shingles first.
6. **Ventilation.** A ventilated attic helps keep the roof deck from trapping heat and moisture, which affects shingle wear and winter ice formation.

## Why Michiana Weather Matters

Northern Indiana and Southwest Michigan put a roof through repeated freeze-thaw cycles, lake-effect snow load, summer heat and severe storms. Shingles that are installed correctly handle that mix well; shingles installed over weak decking or with poor ventilation tend to wear faster. Homes near Lake Michigan, like those in [St. Joseph, MI](/service-areas/st-joseph.html), and inland communities like [South Bend](/service-areas/south-bend.html) all see some version of the same seasonal stress.

Shingle roof cost depends mainly on the size and pitch of the roof, how many layers need to come off, the shingle line you choose, the amount of decking repair needed, and the number of details like valleys, chimneys and skylights. We give you a written estimate that spells these out, and our guide to [what a new roof costs in Michiana](/learning-center/new-roof-cost-michiana.html) explains the factors in more depth.

## Ventilation And Ice Dams In Winter

Attic ventilation is easy to overlook, but in a snowy climate it's one of the details that matters most. A poorly ventilated attic lets heat escape into the roof deck, which can melt snow unevenly and refreeze it at the eaves, and that ice can push water under shingles. Balanced intake and exhaust ventilation, along with ice and water shield at the eaves, helps the roof handle winter. We check ventilation as part of every estimate, and we'll tell you if it needs attention.
