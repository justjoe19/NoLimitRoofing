Gutters only work when water can flow through them. When leaves, seed pods and shingle grit pile up, water spills over the edge, runs down the siding and collects at the foundation. In winter, a gutter full of debris holds water that can freeze and add to ice damming at the roof edge.

Gutter protection is one way to cut down on that. It covers the gutter so rain gets in and leaves stay out. It isn't the right answer for every home, and we'll tell you honestly whether it's the right answer for yours.

## When Gutter Protection Makes Sense

- Large trees hang over or near the roof.
- Your gutters clog often, or you're cleaning them more than you'd like.
- The gutters are high or hard to reach safely.
- Debris-related overflow has already stained the siding or damaged the fascia.

## When It May Not

- There are few or no trees near the house.
- The gutters are already sagging, leaking or poorly pitched. Those need to be repaired or replaced first.
- The roof edge has problems that need attention before anything is covered.

## What To Expect

No product removes the need for any maintenance. Fine debris, pollen and shingle granules can still collect, and guards can need occasional attention. We'll explain what to expect for your property instead of promising a maintenance-free gutter.
