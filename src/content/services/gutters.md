Gutters are easy to overlook until something goes wrong. A section sags, a seam leaks, or a downspout dumps water beside the foundation, and suddenly the problem isn't only the gutter. Water that doesn't get carried away from the house ends up in the fascia, the siding, the landscaping and sometimes the basement.

No Limit Roofing inspects gutters together with the roof edge, because the two work as one system. We explain what's wrong, show you photos, and give you a written estimate before any work begins.

## Signs Your Gutters Need Attention

- **Sagging or pulling away** from the fascia.
- **Leaking seams** or drips along the length of the gutter.
- **Standing water** or plants growing in the gutter.
- **Overflow** during rain, even when the gutters seem clear.
- **Peeling paint or stains** on the fascia or siding below the gutter.
- **Soil erosion or pooling water** near the foundation.

## How Gutters Affect Your Roof

Clogged or failing gutters can push water back under the first row of shingles and soak the fascia and roof edge. In winter, trapped water can freeze and contribute to ice damming, which is one of the leaks behind many roof repair calls. Our guide on [ice and water shield and ventilation](/guides/roof-system-protection.html) explains how the roof edge is protected, and how the gutters fit in.

## Repair Or Replacement

A loose hanger, a failed seam or a short damaged section can often be repaired. Gutters that sag along most of their length, are rusted through, or no longer slope toward the downspouts are usually better replaced. If we think replacement is the better value, we'll show you why. If a repair will do, that's what we'll quote.
