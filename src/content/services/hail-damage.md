Hail is one of the least obvious forms of roof damage — a storm can pass through South Bend, Mishawaka or the surrounding Michiana region without a single shingle visibly out of place from the driveway, while the roof itself has sustained real damage: bruised or cracked shingles, knocked-loose granules, and dented soft metals like vents and flashing. Left unaddressed, that kind of damage shortens the life of the roof and can turn into leaks well before the shingles were expected to wear out.

A hail damage inspection from No Limit Roofing means getting up on the roof and checking for the specific signs hail leaves behind, not just a glance from the ground. We document what we find with photos and explain it in plain language — including telling you honestly if the damage doesn't warrant a claim or repair.

If you do decide to file an insurance claim, we can provide clear documentation of the damage we found to support that process. We're a roofing contractor, not an insurance company, and we won't promise a claim outcome that isn't ours to guarantee — but an accurate, honest inspection is the best starting point either way.

## What Hail Does To A Roof

Hail damage tends to show up as:

- **Bruising.** A soft spot in the shingle where the mat underneath has been fractured, even though the surface looks intact.
- **Granule loss.** Dark, random spots where the protective granules were knocked off, exposing the asphalt.
- **Cracks and punctures.** Especially on older, more brittle shingles.
- **Dents in soft metals.** Vents, flashing, ridge vents and gutters often show impact marks that tell us hail was heavy enough to matter.
- **Collateral signs.** Dents in gutters, downspouts, garage doors or siding can suggest the roof took hits too.

Our guide on [what hail damage looks like on a roof](/learning-center/what-does-hail-damage-look-like.html) shows what to watch for.

## What A Hail Inspection Involves

1. **Ground-level check.** We start with gutters, downspouts and soft metals for signs of impact.
2. **On-roof inspection.** We check the shingles across each slope, looking for hail-specific patterns rather than ordinary wear.
3. **Flashing and vent check.** We look at soft-metal components that show hail strikes.
4. **Photo documentation.** We photograph what we find so you have a clear record.
5. **Plain-language explanation.** We tell you what the damage means and what your options are.

The inspection is free, and it comes with a free written estimate if repair or replacement is recommended.

## Repair, Replacement Or Nothing

Not every hailstorm calls for a new roof. Depending on what we find, the honest answer may be:

- **No action needed.** The roof is fine.
- **A targeted [roof repair](/roofing/roof-repair.html).** Damage is limited and the rest of the roof is sound.
- **[Replacement](/roofing/roof-replacement.html).** Damage is widespread across the roof, or the roof was already near the end of its life.

We'll tell you which one it is, and why.

## Working With Insurance

Homeowners often ask us how the insurance process works. Here's what we can and can't do:

- We can inspect the roof and document damage with photos.
- We can explain what we found in plain terms to you, and give you a written estimate.
- We cannot approve a claim or guarantee one will be paid. That decision belongs to your carrier.

Most policies have a window for filing after a storm, so it's worth having the roof checked reasonably soon after a hailstorm you know hit your area. Check your own policy for the specifics.

## What To Do After A Hailstorm

1. **Look around safely from the ground.** Check gutters, downspouts, window screens, siding and any soft metal for dents or marks.
2. **Photograph what you see,** including hailstones if any are left, with something next to them for scale.
3. **Don't climb on the roof.** Wet or damaged shingles are slippery and dangerous.
4. **Schedule an inspection** so the roof can be checked properly, and in good time.
5. **Read your policy** so you know how claims and deadlines work in your case.
