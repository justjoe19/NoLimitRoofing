Not every roof problem is visible from the ground, and not every homeowner needs to become an expert in shingle wear to know whether their roof is in good shape — that's what an inspection is for. No Limit Roofing's approach is simple: we inspect it, we explain it, and you decide what to do next. There's no pressure to book work you don't need, and no vague answers when you do.

A typical inspection covers accessible shingles, flashing around chimneys and walls, roof penetrations like vents and pipes, ridges and valleys, gutters, and visible decking condition — the places small problems actually start. If we find something worth addressing, we'll explain what it is, why it matters, and what it would take to fix, as part of a free written estimate. If the roof checks out, we'll tell you that too.

Inspections are useful on a regular schedule (catching wear before it becomes a leak) and especially after a storm, when hail or wind damage isn't always obvious from a ladder or the ground. No Limit Roofing has performed inspections across the Michiana region since 2010, and every one is free, with no obligation.

## What We Look For

- **Shingle condition.** Curling, cracking, missing pieces, bare spots where granules have worn away, and signs of hail bruising.
- **Flashing.** The metal and sealant details where the roof meets a chimney, wall, skylight or another roof plane. Failed flashing is among the most common leak sources.
- **Penetrations.** Vent boots, pipes and exhaust vents, whose rubber and sealant wear out before the shingles around them.
- **Ridges and valleys.** Places where water concentrates and wind gets under material.
- **Decking condition.** What we can see or feel of the deck, including soft spots or sagging.
- **Gutters and drainage.** Clogged or damaged gutters that let water back up at the roof edge.
- **Storm indicators.** Dents in soft metals, lifted or creased shingles, and debris damage.

## When To Schedule One

1. **After a hailstorm or high-wind event**, even if the roof looks fine. Damage from [hail](/storm-damage/hail-damage.html) and [wind](/storm-damage/wind-damage.html) is easy to miss from the yard.
2. **Once a year** as a baseline, ideally before winter.
3. **When you notice a stain, a drip or a change in energy bills**, which may point to moisture or ventilation trouble.
4. **When buying or selling a home**, so you know the roof's condition going in.
5. **When the roof is getting older.** Our guide on [how long a roof lasts in Northern Indiana](/learning-center/how-long-does-a-roof-last-in-northern-indiana.html) gives context for what to expect.

## How Michiana Weather Fits In

Freeze-thaw cycles, lake-effect snow, ice at the eaves and summer thunderstorms all age a roof faster than mild climates do. A spring or fall check helps catch what winter or a storm season left behind, before it turns into a leak. Whether you're in [South Bend](/service-areas/south-bend.html), [Goshen](/service-areas/goshen.html) or across the state line in Michigan, the same seasonal wear applies.

## What Happens After The Inspection

1. **We explain what we found.** In plain language, in person, with photos where they help.
2. **We give you options.** That could be no action at all, a targeted [repair](/roofing/roof-repair.html), or, if the roof is past the point repairs make sense, a [replacement](/roofing/roof-replacement.html).
3. **You get a free written estimate** if work is recommended, so you can compare it, think it over and ask questions.
4. **You decide.** There's no obligation and no pressure.

## Inspections And Insurance

If you think a storm damaged your roof, an inspection is the right first step before deciding whether to file a claim. We can document what we find with photos, and we'll tell you honestly if the damage doesn't warrant a claim. Whether a claim is approved is your insurance carrier's decision, not ours.

## Choosing A Roofer With Confidence

An inspection is also a good way to get to know a contractor before committing to a bigger job. Pay attention to whether they explain what they see, put things in writing and pressure you. ## Inspection Doesn't Mean Selling

Some homeowners hesitate to call because they expect a sales pitch. Our inspection is built to give you information, not to close a job. You'll get a plain explanation of the roof's condition, photos when they help, and an honest answer about whether anything needs to be done now, soon or not at all. If we recommend work, it comes as a written estimate you can take your time with.

## Inspections For Home Buyers And Sellers

An inspection can also help when a home is changing hands. Buyers get a clearer picture of what they're taking on, and sellers can find out about problems before a buyer's inspector does. Either way, we'll give you a plain-language explanation of the roof's condition. Ask us about timing if you're working around a closing date.
