Your roof doesn't work alone. The siding, soffit and fascia around it are what close up the edges of the house, direct water away and keep weather and animals out of the walls and attic. When they start to fail, the problem rarely stays small: a soft fascia board lets water reach the roof deck, and a gap in the soffit lets in more than air.

No Limit Roofing inspects siding, soffit and fascia alongside the roof, because the three are so closely connected. We look at what's there, photograph any problem, and explain it in plain language before we recommend anything.

## Signs Your Siding, Soffit Or Fascia Needs Attention

- **Soft, dark or rotted wood** at the roof edge or around windows.
- **Peeling or bubbling paint**, which often means moisture is getting behind the surface.
- **Cracked, warped or loose siding panels.**
- **Gaps in the soffit** where birds, insects or squirrels can get in.
- **Gutters pulling away** from the house, which can mean the fascia behind them is no longer holding.
- **Water stains** on the underside of the overhang or on the walls below the roofline.

## Why They Matter To The Roof

Fascia and soffit are part of the roof edge. The fascia is what the gutters hang from, and the soffit often holds the vents that let the attic breathe. Our guide on [ice and water shield and ventilation](/guides/roof-system-protection.html) explains why that airflow matters to the life of the roof. If the edge is rotted, it's usually best to fix it when the roof is repaired or replaced, while the work is already open.

## Repair Or Replace?

Localized damage, such as a short run of rotted fascia or a few cracked panels, can usually be repaired. Widespread rot, repeated water intrusion or siding that can no longer be matched may point to a larger replacement. We'll tell you which one we think fits your home, and why.
