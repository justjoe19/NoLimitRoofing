A skylight brings light into a room, and it also adds an opening to the roof. That makes it one of the places water is most likely to find a way in. The good news is that a leaking skylight is often fixable without replacing the whole thing, and the cause is frequently the flashing around it, not the skylight.

No Limit Roofing inspects the skylight, its flashing and the roof around it, so we can tell you where the water is actually coming from before recommending any work.

## Common Causes Of Skylight Leaks

- **Worn or pulled-away flashing.** The metal around the skylight seals the joint between the skylight and the roof. When it fails, water gets behind it.
- **Failed sealant or gaskets** that have dried, cracked or shrunk over time.
- **Damaged shingles** around the skylight, from wind, age or hail.
- **Condensation**, which can look like a leak but comes from humidity inside the house.
- **A cracked or fogged skylight**, where the seal between panes or the glazing itself has failed.
- **A leak that started elsewhere** and traveled along the decking to the skylight.

## Repair Or Replace?

If the flashing or sealant is the problem, repair is often enough. If the skylight itself is cracked, leaking through its frame or fogged between the panes, replacement usually makes more sense. Our guide on [repair versus replacement](/guides/repair-vs-replacement.html) explains how we think about that choice for a roof, and the same logic applies here.
