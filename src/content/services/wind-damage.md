High wind is one of the more deceptive causes of roof damage, because it doesn't always leave obvious evidence. A strong gust can crease a shingle, lift its edge and break its adhesive seal, or work flashing loose around a vent or chimney — all without a single shingle actually blowing off the roof. From the ground, everything can look completely normal, while the roof's ability to shed water has already been compromised.

No Limit Roofing inspects for the specific signs wind damage leaves behind: creased or lifted shingles, disturbed flashing, and loosened vents or penetrations, in addition to the more obvious signs like missing shingles or exposed decking. If we find damage, we'll explain exactly what happened and what it will take to fix, and in most cases a targeted repair is enough — a full replacement isn't automatically the answer.

If your area has recently had a high-wind event, a free inspection is worth scheduling even without visible damage from the ground. ## How Wind Damages Shingles

Asphalt shingles are held down by an adhesive strip that seals each shingle to the one below it. Wind can attack that seal in several ways:

- **Lifting.** Gusts get under a shingle's edge and break the seal. The shingle may settle back down, but it's no longer bonded.
- **Creasing.** A shingle folds back and creases across its width, weakening the mat.
- **Tearing and missing shingles.** The most visible kind of damage, often along ridges, rakes and eaves.
- **Loosened flashing and vents.** Wind can work metal and rubber components loose, opening a path for water.

Unsealed shingles are more likely to lift or blow off in the next storm, and water can get underneath them. Our guide on [how wind damages shingles without blowing them off](/learning-center/can-wind-damage-shingles-without-blowing-off.html) goes into more detail.

## Where Wind Damage Shows Up First

Wind hits a roof unevenly. The most exposed areas usually go first:

1. **Ridges and hips**, where the roof meets the wind directly
2. **Rakes and eaves**, the exposed edges of the roof
3. **Corners and slopes facing the prevailing wind**
4. **Flashing at chimneys, walls and vents**
5. **Older shingles** whose adhesive strips have weakened with age

## What A Wind Damage Inspection Includes

3. **Flashing and penetration check** at chimneys, vents, pipes and walls
4. **Ridge and edge check**, the most exposed parts of the roof

## Repair Or Replacement

Most wind damage is localized, so a targeted repair usually makes sense when the decking and the rest of the roof are sound. That could mean replacing damaged shingles, resealing or replacing flashing, or securing vents. Replacement enters the picture when damage is widespread or the roof was already near the end of its life. ## What To Do After A Windstorm

1. **Check the yard** for shingle pieces, branches and debris, which can point to roof damage.
2. **Look at the roofline from the ground** for shingles that are missing, lifted or sitting unevenly.
3. **Check the attic and ceilings** for new stains or damp insulation.
4. **Photograph any damage** from a safe distance.
5. **Call for an inspection** even if you don't see anything, since creased and unsealed shingles are easy to miss.

We would rather look at a roof that turns out to be fine than have you find a leak in the next rain.
